01 · High-yield review

The cram sheet

Memorize the frameworks, then practice applying them. These are the most quiz-ready distinctions across the materials.

Brand equity

CBBE pyramid

  1. Salience: Who are you? (identity)
  2. Performance + imagery: What are you? (meaning)
  3. Judgments + feelings: What about you? (response)
  4. Resonance: What about you and me? (relationship)
Goal setting

OKRs

Objective = what. Significant, concrete, action-oriented, inspirational.

Key results = how/how much. 2–5 measurable, time-bound, verifiable outcomes.

Measure outcomes, not completed activity. Avoid vanity metrics.
Virality

Designed sharing

Identity × Emotion × Participation

Virality is behavior built into an experience that people choose to share. Because the formula multiplies, one zero kills it.

Trust & community

Relationship is the moat

Currency stack: Value → Attention → Trust → Relationship → Belonging.

Five trusts: functional, transactional, moral, societal, personal.

Community creates long-term collective value; it is not merely a conversion channel.

Growth

Early customers

Do things that don't scale: recruit manually, install personally, delight intensely, learn quickly.

Collison installation: don't send the signup link—take the laptop and install the product now.

AI discovery

SEO → GEO

SEO: rank links, earn clicks, measure CTR.

GEO: become a cited source inside generated answers, measure reference rate.

Make content structured, attributable, specific, and easy for models to extract.

Synthetic research

A wind tunnel, not proof

Synthetic audiences simulate evidence-grounded perspectives for fast, directional message testing.

Every detail needs a source, assumption label, or open question. Validate high-stakes conclusions with real people.

Purpose

Start with Why

WHY → HOW → WHAT. Inspiring brands communicate from the inside out.

“What” maps to the rational neocortex; “Why” maps to the limbic system, where trust, loyalty, and decisions live.

Gen Alpha

Gateway generation

Family CMO, content over clout, always-on gamification, and AI as a “third parent.”

Key implication: the creator may be replaceable; the relatable, repeatable format is not.

03 · Deep review

Concept modules

Open a module when you need the full framework, examples, and likely exam angles.

Brand equity, identity, and narrative

CBBE: build upward

Strong brands move from awareness to meaning, response, and finally active loyalty. The left side is more logical (performance, judgments); the right side is more emotional (imagery, feelings). The strongest brands use both.

Resonance drivers: consistent performance; positive judgments and feelings; brand community; co-creation; alignment with identity and values.

Collaboration logic: a useful partner lends equity and fills a weak part of the pyramid with new imagery. Similar partners may add little.

Brand pillars and narrative

Five pillar categories: purpose/mission, perception/positioning, identity/personality, values/beliefs, experience standards.

Whydentity YOU: origin, obsession, beliefs, mission, archetype, proof.

Whydentity THEM: functional (do), emotional (feel), self-expressive (be).

Niche: audience, territory, context, tension/stakes.

Applied cases

  • Indeed: mission (“We help people get jobs”) flows into values, accessible visuals, sonic branding, and environmental touchpoints. Identity is more than a logo.
  • Louis Vuitton: controlled distribution, heritage codes, cultural partnerships, experiential retail, and engineered scarcity preserve exclusivity while sustaining visibility.
  • Start With Why: Apple communicates belief before product. People buy why you do it, not only what you do.
  • Content marketing: create and distribute valuable, relevant, consistent content for a clearly defined audience to drive profitable customer action.
Application prompt: Diagnose a weak brand by locating the missing CBBE layer, then propose a pillar or narrative move that repairs it.
Startup growth, acquisition, and loyalty

First customers: manual before scalable

  • YC founders repeatedly use cold outreach, personal networks, communities, discovery calls, and bespoke demos.
  • Paul Graham: startups do not launch themselves. Recruit users, deliver a shockingly good experience, and compound small weekly growth.
  • Bullseye: brainstorm channels → rank → run cheap tests → focus on the winner.
  • Charge early: payment provides stronger evidence of demand than free interest.

CAC is not one number

Blended CAC = total acquisition spend ÷ total customers. But the cheap audience is finite; marginal CAC rises as each additional block becomes harder to reach. Buy the cheapest next block, not the channel with the cheapest historical average.

Forerunner's loyalty thesis: acquisition grew expensive and over-optimized, so durable growth must invest in LTV, service, access, rewards, and retention. Limitation: loyalty has less leverage in very low-frequency categories.

Funnel and ladder

Funnel: TOFU awareness → MOFU consideration → BOFU decision → retention and advocacy. It is not perfectly linear.

Value ladder: free content → tripwire → core product → upsell.

Application prompt: If average CAC is low but the next cohort is expensive and churn is high, recommend a marginal-CAC test plus a loyalty intervention.
OKRs: alignment, measurement, and impact

SMART (course version)

Specific · Measurable · Actionable · Realistic · Time-bound.

Five superpowers

Focus on ~3 quarterly objectives · align the team · check in weekly · distinguish stretch from commit · measure outcomes.

Objective levels

Strategic (top-level) · tactical (milestones) · operational (individual/process).

Stretch vs. commit: a stretch OKR deliberately extends the organization; a committed OKR must be achieved. Lead vs. lag: a lead measure is an input you control; a lag measure is the result that follows.

Cases

  • Allbirds: connected mission and finance through sustainability OKRs (“KIWIs”—Keep Improving With Intent), life-cycle measurement, and carbon neutrality.
  • Superhuman: focused product-market fit around the share of users who would be “very disappointed” without the product.
  • Stitch Fix: shifted from “did we launch?” to “what impact did the launch create?” Company OKRs cascaded to teams and increased cross-functional collaboration.
Weak KR: “Publish 10 posts.” Stronger KR: “Increase qualified organic leads 20% by quarter end.” The first is output; the second is outcome.
PR, positioning, and message design

Course framing: PR is the personal relationships a brand has with audiences. Journalists create content their audiences value; they are not obligated to promote your startup.

Talk-track circles

  • Company: problem solved, customer evidence, company/talent best practices.
  • Industry: six-month and two-year outlook, tailwinds, obstacles, peer views.
  • Experience: founder fit, upbringing, skills, interests.
  • Sweet spot: the credible overlap of all three.

Value proposition formulas

[Company] is developing [product] to help [audience] [solve a problem] with [secret sauce].

Or: [Company] created [product] for [audience] that results in [value 1], [value 2], [value 3].

Press kit: mission, story, fact sheet, assets, bios/contact, release; optionally awards, demos, FAQs, coverage. A release needs the five Ws, a hook, concept-first lead, evidence, useful links, and no jargon.

Measurement: reach, impressions, share of voice, message pull-through; plus business impact, sentiment, resonance, coverage quality, and relationship strength.

Community, trust, experience, and conversion

Go-to-community is not go-to-market

Marketing often serves near-term funnel goals. Community creates long-term trust, member-to-member value, switching costs, and a potential moat. Types:

  • Product: centered on a product and its users.
  • Practice: centered on advancing a discipline or craft.
  • Play: centered on a shared interest or hobby.

Receptional design

Design for how an experience is received in a user's need state, not only how its creator conceives it. “Automagical” micro-moments can transform emotion: serotonin (anxiety falls), dopamine (reward), endorphins (energy), oxytocin (trust/love).

Loyalty Love Loop ingredients: purpose and values, tribe/spotlights, storytelling, receptional design, gamification, IRL/virtual worlds, network effects, engineered sharing, co-creation, phygital commerce, and advocacy.

Ethical conversion

Master entry and exit points: align landing-page promise, load quickly, clarify one value proposition, show the CTA, reduce checkout friction, support autofill and familiar payment methods.

Use ethical influence—social proof, endowment, customer-as-hero—not fake timers, false discounts, or manufactured scarcity. UX non-negotiables: clarity, consistency, error prevention.

Virality, storytelling, and creator growth

Six viral mechanics

  • Participation ritual: belonging and FOMO.
  • Personalization: identity expression.
  • Conflict/sides: tribal loyalty and outrage.
  • Brand character: entertainment.
  • Real-time relevance: cultural credibility.
  • Creative tool: the audience becomes distribution.

Algorithm model

  1. Analyze content: words, on-screen text, visuals, audio, topic.
  2. Analyze profile: who engages and account standing.
  3. Test distribution: micro audience → larger audience → masses.

Course IG targets: skip rate under 40%; share rate over 6%; average watch time over 21 seconds; 50% retention after five seconds. Treat these as course heuristics, not universal platform guarantees.

Creator playbook

Recruit creators on new accounts → warm up accounts → post 1–3 videos daily → pay on proven performance (course example: CPM). Make the product the viral feature. Avoid flat fees before proving format, reach-only celebrity bets, and skipped warm-up.

Story tools

Hook, open loop, callback, rule of three, contrast, audience participation, signposting, ethos/logos/pathos, pattern interrupt, metaphor, and call to action. Use them judiciously.

Story chemistry framework: tension can produce cortisol/focus; vulnerability and connection support oxytocin; reward/reveal supports dopamine; humor supports endorphins.

AI discovery and synthetic audiences

GEO replaces a link-first mindset

Traditional SEO optimizes ranking and click-through. GEO optimizes the chance that an AI system cites or synthesizes your material. “Model relevance” and reference rate become important. Structure, specificity, original evidence, clear summaries, and machine-readable meaning increase usefulness.

Why AI defaults to average

Generative AI predicts likely continuations from patterns, so generic prompts invite generic center-of-distribution answers. Specific context pushes output into more useful tails. Use AI for assembly so humans can spend more time on judgment.

Build a synthetic audience

  1. Choose the question.
  2. Map the culture.
  3. Gather evidence.
  4. Draft several perspectives.
  5. Rehearse the message.
  6. Check with real people and improve.

Three lenses: qualitative, quantitative, systems thinking. Four test questions: What is this? How would you explain it to a friend? What is unclear or missing? What might stop you?

Digiday distinction: synthetic research/data is the umbrella; a synthetic audience is a panel trained on human behavior data. “Cohort boosting” expands a smaller real dataset into a larger modeled sample.

Decision rule: synthetic tools for fast directional clarity; high-fidelity models for time-pressured behavioral hypotheses; recruited humans for observation, regulation, major investment, and final validation.
Gen Alpha and the attention economy

Five Gen Alpha themes

  1. Adultification: youth increasingly act as the “Family CMO,” shaping household discovery and decisions.
  2. Content over clout: relevance, surprise, and relatability can matter more than celebrity.
  3. Always-on game: streaks, points, badges, and early access strongly shape behavior—requiring ethical guardrails.
  4. AI as a third parent: AI supports play, learning, homework, creativity, and sometimes personal advice.
  5. Influence means responsibility: disclose sponsorship, respect autonomy, avoid dark patterns and compulsive design.

The ACC × USC study combined real surveys/focus groups and synthetic personas. Its reported synthetic model aligned with real responses 96% of the time, but the methodology itself reinforces the need to compare modeled and real evidence.

Attention and super users

Activate's 2026 outlook frames attention as heavily multitasked. “Super Users” are a minority with disproportionate media time, spending, early adoption, subscriptions, advocacy, and comfort sharing data. Strategic implication: not all audience members have equal behavior or value.

Synthesis prompt: Design a Gen Alpha campaign that uses a repeatable format and ethical gamification, then explain how you would test it with synthetic and real audiences.

05 · Written-response practice

Essay and short-answer quiz

Brand equity

Explain how a brand moves from salience to resonance in the CBBE pyramid. Diagnose what happens when one middle layer is weak.

Strong answer outline

  • Define identity → meaning → response → relationship.
  • Explain performance/imagery and judgments/feelings as the two logical/emotional paths.
  • Show that weak meaning prevents favorable responses and resonance.
  • Apply a collaboration, community, or co-creation tactic to repair the weak layer.
Purpose & identity

Compare Sinek's Golden Circle with brand pillars and Whydentity. How do they work together to produce a coherent brand?

Strong answer outline

  • Golden Circle: Why → How → What; belief precedes product.
  • Brand pillars operationalize purpose through positioning, personality, values, and experience.
  • Whydentity YOU establishes origin, belief, mission, archetype, and proof; THEM connects functional, emotional, and self-expressive value.
  • Explain consistency between internal purpose and external customer meaning.
Startup growth

Why should an early startup do things that do not scale? Explain when it should shift toward structured channel testing.

Strong answer outline

  • Manual recruiting and “Collison installation” create learning, trust, and initial momentum.
  • Delighting early users reveals needs and builds a culture of service.
  • Small growth compounds; premature automation can scale the wrong assumptions.
  • Shift toward Bullseye testing after enough customer insight exists: brainstorm → rank → test → focus.
Growth economics

Why is blended CAC insufficient for growth decisions? Connect marginal CAC to loyalty and LTV.

Strong answer outline

  • Blended CAC averages past spend across all customers.
  • Marginal CAC measures the cost of the next customer block and rises as cheap audiences run out.
  • Choose the cheapest next increment, not the cheapest historical channel.
  • Loyalty improves retention/LTV and reduces dependence on increasingly expensive acquisition, though category frequency limits its power.
OKRs

Design an effective marketing OKR and explain why it is stronger than a list of outputs or vanity metrics.

Strong answer outline

  • Objective: significant, concrete, action-oriented, inspirational “what.”
  • Give 2–5 measurable, time-bound, verifiable key results.
  • Distinguish controllable lead measures from lag outcomes.
  • Contrast “publish 10 posts” with an outcome such as qualified leads, retention, or message pull-through.
  • Explain alignment, weekly check-ins, and stretch versus committed goals.
Community

Why is community not merely marketing? Explain how trust, network effects, and belonging can create defensibility.

Strong answer outline

  • Marketing often optimizes message, reach, and near-term conversion.
  • Community creates member-to-member value and durable relationships.
  • Use the currency stack: value → attention → trust → relationship → belonging.
  • Network effects can increase utility, reduce acquisition dependence, raise switching costs, and create a moat.
  • Distinguish product, practice, and play communities.
Virality

Explain why virality must be engineered into the experience rather than added as promotion after launch.

Strong answer outline

  • Use Identity × Emotion × Participation; one zero can collapse sharing.
  • Sharing must give the user a social or expressive payoff.
  • Apply mechanics such as ritual, personalization, creative tools, or real-time relevance.
  • Separate designed participation from paid reach or one large influencer.
  • Connect to algorithm testing: content/profile understanding precedes wider distribution.
AI research

When should a marketer use synthetic audiences, and when must real-human research remain central?

Strong answer outline

  • Define synthetic audiences as evidence-grounded simulated perspectives—a communication wind tunnel.
  • Use them for fast directional testing, concept narrowing, and message rehearsal.
  • Label every important detail as source, assumption, or open question.
  • Use real humans for observed behavior, emotional nuance, high-stakes investment, regulation, and final validation.
  • Conclude “synthetic and human,” not “synthetic or human.”
Discovery

Compare SEO and GEO. How must content strategy and measurement change when answers replace lists of links?

Strong answer outline

  • SEO optimizes ranking and clicks; GEO optimizes citation and synthesis in generated answers.
  • CTR becomes less complete; reference rate/model relevance becomes important.
  • Content needs clear structure, specificity, original evidence, attribution, and extractable summaries.
  • Explain the zero-click challenge and why discoverability can occur without a site visit.
Gen Alpha

Design an ethical Gen Alpha campaign using “content over clout” and gamification. How would you test it?

Strong answer outline

  • Use a relatable, surprising, repeatable format rather than celebrity alone.
  • Build age-appropriate participation without manipulative streaks or dark patterns.
  • Disclose sponsorship and preserve autonomy.
  • Use synthetic audiences for early message rehearsal, then validate comprehension, behavior, and emotional response with youth and parents.
Experience

Connect receptional design with ethical conversion. How can a brand reduce friction without manipulating customers?

Strong answer outline

  • Receptional design starts from the user's need state and desired emotional transformation.
  • Use clarity, consistency, error prevention, autofill, familiar payments, and a coherent entry/exit journey.
  • Apply ethical social proof, endowment, and customer-as-hero framing.
  • Reject false urgency, false scarcity, and deceptive pricing because they damage trust.
Integrated strategy

A new brand has awareness but weak loyalty. Build an integrated response using CBBE, content, community, OKRs, and measurement.

Strong answer outline

  • Diagnose the weak CBBE layer: meaning, response, or relationship—not salience.
  • Use valuable, relevant, consistent content to clarify meaning and earn trust.
  • Create community/co-creation to move from audience to belonging.
  • Set an outcome OKR around retention, advocacy, participation, or relationship strength.
  • Measure lead behaviors plus lag results; avoid reach-only vanity metrics.
